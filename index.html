Lesson 1: Phonetics

1. Introduction to Pinyin & Syllables

Each Chinese character usually represents a single syllable. For example, the character 你 (you) has the Pinyin nǐ.

The Components of Pinyin

A Chinese syllable is made up of three parts:

  • Initial: The consonantal beginning of a syllable.
  • Final: The part of the syllable excluding the initial.
  • Tone: One of the 4 tones (or neutral tone) placed on the main vowel.
n + i + ˇ = nǐ

2. The Four Tones

Different Tones may express entirely different meanings.
For example: mā (mother) vs. mà (scold).

🎭 Teacher's Tip: Be DRAMATIC!
To pronounce tones accurately, you must exaggerate your facial expressions and head movements! Use your hand to draw the tone shapes in the air while speaking. Don't be shy!

5. Pinyin Spelling Rules (The Easy Way!)

🛡️ Rule #1: The "Y & W" Bodyguards

The vowels i and u are shy! If they are the first letter of a syllable, they need a bodyguard.

  • If i is alone, add "y" in front:
    i → yi
  • If i has friends, turn it into "y":
    ian → yan
  • If u is alone, add "w" in front:
    u → wu
  • If u has friends, turn it into "w":
    uan → wan

🎩 Rule #2: The Magic Disappearing Dots

The initials j, q, x are very strict! When the vowel ü meets them, it must take off its "hat" (the two dots) out of respect.

j + ü = ju (Not jü)
q + ü = qu
x + ü = xu

⚠️ Note: j, q, and x NEVER hang out with normal u, a, o, or e. If you see "ju", it is always pronounced as "jü"!

Also, if ü has no initial consonant, add "y" and drop the dots (e.g., üan → yuan).

👑 Rule #3: Who gets the Tone Crown?

When there are multiple vowels, who wears the Tone Crown? They line up by mouth size:

a > o > e > i > u > ü

The vowel that opens the mouth the widest gets the mark! (e.g., in bāi, "a" gets it; in guó, "o" gets it).
Except: For the twins iu and ui, the crown always goes to the LAST vowel (e.g., tuī, qiú).

✂️ Rule #4: The Syllable Slicer (')

Sometimes, syllables crash into each other. We use an apostrophe (') to slice them apart so we don't read it wrong.

Tiān + ān + mén = Tiān'ānmén (Not Tiananmen)
shí + èr = shí'èr
