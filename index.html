KUAILE XUE HANYU: Lesson 1

The Phonetics of Chinese (Teacher's Edition)

Target Audience: Zero-basis beginners (Malay, Sabahan, Indian students)

Total Duration: 2.5 Hours (150 minutes)

0:00 - 0:15 (15 mins)

1. Introduction to Pinyin & Syllables

Concept: Each Chinese character usually represents a single syllable[cite: 1].

Explain that a Chinese syllable is made up of three parts[cite: 1]:

  • Initial: The consonantal beginning of a syllable[cite: 1].
  • Final: The part of the syllable excluding the initial[cite: 1].
  • Tone: 4 tones and a neutral tone[cite: 1].

Example on board: n + ǐ = nǐ (你)[cite: 1].

0:15 - 0:45 (30 mins)

2. The Four Tones

Different tones express different meanings. E.g., mā (mother) vs. mà (scold)[cite: 1, 2].

  • 1st Tone (Flat tone): Sounds like a robot (ēēēē)[cite: 2].
  • 2nd Tone (Rising tone): Sounds confused (like asking "Huh?")[cite: 2].
  • 3rd Tone (Falling-Rising tone): Like a small rollercoaster (your voice goes down, then up)[cite: 2].
  • 4th Tone (Falling tone): Sounds angry OR sounds like you got stepped on![cite: 2].
  • Neutral Tone: Light, short pronunciation used in continuous speech[cite: 2]. It is seldom used to stand alone[cite: 2].

Activity: Have students practice tones on the syllable "ma" until they can differentiate the "robot" from the "angry" tone.

0:45 - 1:15 (30 mins)

3. Simple Finals (Vowels) with Malay/English Hacks

Finals' pronunciation is determined by tongue height, tongue position, and lip shape[cite: 1]. Introduce the "Big mouth", "Smile", and "Round lip" concepts[cite: 2].

Pinyin Final Mouth Shape Pronunciation Hacks (Suku Kata / English)
a Open Mouth (Big mouth sound)[cite: 2] BM - abang | BI - father[cite: 2]
o Open Mouth[cite: 2] BM - otak | BI - law[cite: 2]
e Open Mouth[cite: 2] BM - emak | BI - ago[cite: 2]
i Spread Vowel (Smile sound)[cite: 2] BM - ikan | BI - see[cite: 2]
u Rounded Vowel (Round lip sound)[cite: 2] BM - ulang | BI - mood[cite: 2]
ü Close Rounded (Tight round lip sound)[cite: 2] BM - yoyo | BI - salut[cite: 2]
Teacher Tip: Spend extra time on "ü". Have students say "i" (ikan) but force their lips into the "u" (ulang) shape.
1:15 - 1:25 (10 mins)

Short Break

1:25 - 2:00 (35 mins)

4. Initials (Consonants) & The "Suku Kata" Shortcuts

Chinese pinyin has 21 initials[cite: 1].

A. Simple Initials

  • b, p, m, f: Both lips / upper teeth and lower lip[cite: 1, 2]. Think: bear, pear, may, food[cite: 2].
  • d, t, n, l: Tongue tip / nose sound / side sound[cite: 1, 2]. Think: do, two, night, light[cite: 2].
  • g, k, h: Back of the tongue[cite: 1, 2]. Think: bag, back, hat[cite: 2].

B. Palatal Initials (j, q, x) vs Sibilant Initials (z, c, s)

This is often the hardest part for Malaysian students. Use these direct comparisons:

Pinyin Category Suku Kata / English Hack Examples
j Palatal (front of tongue)[cite: 1, 2] BM - zirafah / BI - chicken[cite: 2] jī (chicken)[cite: 2]
q Palatal[cite: 1, 2] BM - cita[cite: 2] qī (seven)[cite: 2]
x Palatal[cite: 1, 2] BM - siput[cite: 2] xī (west)[cite: 2]
z Sibilant (tongue tip)[cite: 1, 2] BI - kids[cite: 2] zū (to rent)[cite: 2]
c Sibilant[cite: 1, 2] BI - cats[cite: 2] cí (word)[cite: 2]
s Sibilant[cite: 1, 2] BI - speak[cite: 2] sì (four)[cite: 2]
CRITICAL WARNING FOR MALAY STUDENTS:
Make sure they do NOT read "z" as "zirafah", "c" as "cita", or "s" as "siput"[cite: 2]. Remind them:
❌ zī ≠ zirafah --> ✅ jī = zirafah[cite: 2]
❌ cī ≠ cita --> ✅ qī = cita[cite: 2]
❌ sī ≠ siput --> ✅ xī = siput[cite: 2]

C. Retroflex Initials (zh, ch, sh, r)

Tongue tip curled back[cite: 1, 2]. Think: zh (page), ch (cheap), sh (sheep), r (right)[cite: 2].

2:00 - 2:15 (15 mins)

5. Compound & Nasal Finals (Traps to Avoid)

Explain that "ian" does NOT sound like "ee-an". It sounds like "yen" or "ien"[cite: 2]. (e.g., piányi, yán)[cite: 2].

Similarly, "üan" is pronounced like "yuen" (e.g., yuán)[cite: 2].

Nasal Finals: -n sounds like BI "win" (e.g., fàn), while -ng sounds like BI "wing" (e.g., fàng)[cite: 2].

2:15 - 2:25 (10 mins)

6. Essential Spelling Rules

  • The "y" and "w" Rules:
    • If "i" is the main vowel, add "y" (i → yi)[cite: 1]. If not the main vowel, replace with "y" (ian → yan)[cite: 1, 2].
    • If "u" is the main vowel, add "w" (u → wu)[cite: 1, 2]. Replace "u" with "w" for others (uan → wan)[cite: 1, 2].
  • Dropping the Dots on "ü": When "ü" combines with "j", "q", and "x", the two dots are OMITTED (jü → ju, qü → qu, xü → xu)[cite: 1, 2]. Note: "j, q, x" never combine with a, o, e, u[cite: 1, 2]. Also, when "ü" has no initial consonant, add "y" and remove the dots (üan → yuan)[cite: 2].
  • Tone Marks: The tone mark must be placed on the main vowel letters in order of a, o, e, i, u, ü[cite: 1, 2]. When marked above "i", the dot is omitted (e.g., yín)[cite: 1].
  • Dividing Mark ('): Used when a syllable starting with "a, o, e" follows another syllable to clarify boundaries (e.g., Tian'anmén vs Tiananmen)[cite: 1, 2].
  • Capitalization: Capitalize the first letter of proper nouns (e.g., Lín Nà) and the first letter of a sentence[cite: 1, 2]. Syllables forming the same word should be written together[cite: 1].
2:25 - 2:30 (5 mins)
