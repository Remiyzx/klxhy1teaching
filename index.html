1. Introduction to Pinyin & Syllables KLXHY pg 1

Each Chinese character usually represents a single syllable. For example, the character 你 (you) has the Pinyin nǐ.

The Components of Pinyin

A Chinese syllable is made up of three parts:

  • Initial: The consonantal beginning of a syllable.
  • Final: The part of the syllable excluding the initial.
  • Tone: One of the 4 tones (or neutral tone) placed on the main vowel.
n + i + ˇ = nǐ

2. The Four Tones KLXHY pg 3 - 4

Different Tones may express entirely different meanings.
For example: mā (mother) vs. mà (scold).

6. Complete Pinyin Spelling Rules KLXHY pg 4 - 5

🛡️ Rule #1: The "Y & W" Bodyguards (Zero Initial Syllables) KLXHY pg 4

💡 Why can ONLY "a, o, e" stand alone without Y/W?
• a, o, e are "Big Mouth / High-Volume Vowels". They are strong and clear enough to form independent syllables by themselves without needing Y or W bodyguards! (e.g., ā 啊, ò 哦, è 饿).
• i, u, ü have tighter mouth shapes, so they need Y or W as sound separators to mark the start of a syllable.

When a syllable starts with i, u, or ü without a consonant initial, add its bodyguard:

1. Starting with "i":
• i is Alone: Add y in front → i → yi, in → yin, ing → ying
• i Has Vowel Friends: Replace i with y → ia → ya, ian → yan, iou → you
2. Starting with "u":
• u is Alone: Add w in front → u → wu
• u Has Vowel Friends: Replace u with w → ua → wa, uan → wan, uei → wei
3. Starting with "ü":
• Always add y in front AND drop the two dots → ü → yu, üe → yue, üan → yuan, ün → yun
⚡ Shrinking Finals Formula: When preceded by an initial consonant, three finals drop their middle vowel to save space:
iou → -iu (e.g. x + iou = xiu)  |  uei → -ui (e.g. d + uei = dui)  |  uen → -un (e.g. t + uen = tun)

🎩 Rule #2: Dropping Dots on "ü" KLXHY pg 4 - 5

🤔 WHY do we drop the dots?
Because j, q, x NEVER combine with the plain vowel "u"! Since there is zero possibility of confusing "ü" with "u" after j, q, x, the two dots are redundant, so we drop them to make writing faster!

When the vowel ü combines with j, q, x, the two dots over it are OMITTED:

Initials ü üe üan ün
j ju jue juan jun
q qu que quan qun
x xu xue xuan xun
⚠️ WHY do we KEEP the dots for n & l?
Because n and l CAN combine with BOTH "u" and "ü"! We must keep the dots to distinguish them (e.g., l + ü = lǚ green vs l + u = lǔ land).

👑 Rule #3: Who Gets the Tone Crown? (6 Complete Rules) KLXHY pg 5

When placing tone marks, the vowel with the biggest mouth opens the widest to wear the crown!

1. Single Vowel

Mark directly above it (e.g., mā).

2. Two Vowels

Follow order a > o > e > i > u > ü (e.g., bāi, guó).

3. Twin Twins (iu & ui)

Crown goes on the LAST vowel (tuī, qiú).

4. Three Vowels

Crown goes on the MIDDLE vowel (guǎi).

5. Dot-Free "i"

Omit dot above "i" when crowned (e.g., yín).

6. Neutral Tone

No tone mark above neutral syllables (e.g., bàba).

☁️ Neutral Tone Usage: Common family words (māma, bàba), grammatical particles (de, le, ma), repeated verbs (kànkan), compound words (péngyou).

✂️ Rule #4: The Syllable Slicer (') KLXHY pg 5

When a syllable starting with a, o, e follows another syllable, use an apostrophe (') in between to avoid confusion over syllable boundaries!

✍️ Rule #5: Word Glue & Big Letters KLXHY pg 5

📐 How to Count Chinese Syllables:

In Mandarin, 1 Chinese Character = 1 Spoken Syllable = 1 Pinyin Unit. Every single syllable you speak counts as 1 syllable!

Wǒ(1) bà(2)ba(3) mā(4)ma(5) hěn(6) máng(7). → 7 Syllables total!
