1. Introduction to Pinyin & Syllables

Each Chinese character usually represents a single syllable. For example, the character 你 (you) has the Pinyin nǐ.

The Components of Pinyin

A Chinese syllable is made up of three parts:

  • Initial: The consonantal beginning of a syllable.
  • Final: The part of the syllable excluding the initial.
  • Tone: One of the 4 tones (or neutral tone) placed on the main vowel.
n + i + ˇ = nǐ

2. The Four Tones

Different Tones may express entirely different meanings.
For example: mā (mother) vs. mà (scold).

🎭 Teacher's Tip: Be DRAMATIC!
To pronounce tones accurately, you must exaggerate your facial expressions and head movements! Use your hand to draw the tone shapes in the air while speaking. Don't be shy!

5. Complete Pinyin Spelling Rules

🛡️ Rule #1: The "Y & W" Bodyguards (Zero Initial Syllables)

The vowels i, u, and ü are shy! When forming standalone syllables without a consonant initial, they need a bodyguard ("y" or "w"):

i Alone i → yi
in → yin
ing → ying
i Has Friends ia → ya
ian → yan
iou → you
u Alone u → wu
u Has Friends ua → wa
uan → wan
uei → wei
ü Alone / Friends ü → yu
üe → yue
üan → yuan
ün → yun
⚡ Shrinking Finals Formula: When preceded by an initial consonant, three finals drop their middle vowel to save space:
iou → -iu (e.g. x + iou = xiu)  |  uei → -ui (e.g. d + uei = dui)  |  uen → -un (e.g. t + uen = tun)

🎩 Rule #2: Dropping Dots on "ü"

When the vowel ü combines with j, q, x, the two dots over it are OMITTED:

Initials ü üe üan ün
j ju jue juan jun
q qu que quan qun
x xu xue xuan xun
⚠️ Crucial Notes:
• j, q, x NEVER combine directly with simple vowels a, o, e, u.
• Two dots of "ü" REMAIN UNCHANGED when spelled with initials n and l because they can combine with both "ü" and "u" (e.g., l + ü = lǚ vs l + u = lǔ).

👑 Rule #3: Who Gets the Tone Crown? (6 Complete Rules)

When placing tone marks, the vowel with the biggest mouth opens the widest to wear the crown!

1. Single Vowel

Mark directly above it (e.g., mā).

2. Two Vowels

Follow order a > o > e > i > u > ü (e.g., bāi, guó).

3. Twin Twins (iu & ui)

Crown goes on the LAST vowel (tuī, qiú).

4. Three Vowels

Crown goes on the MIDDLE vowel (guǎi).

5. Dot-Free "i"

Omit dot above "i" when crowned (e.g., yín).

6. Neutral Tone

No tone mark above neutral syllables (e.g., bàba).

☁️ Neutral Tone Usage: Common family words (māma, bàba), grammatical particles (de, le, ma), repeated verbs (kànkan), compound words (péngyou).

✂️ Rule #4: The Syllable Slicer (')

When a syllable starting with a, o, e follows another syllable, use an apostrophe (') in between to avoid confusion over syllable boundaries!

Tiānānmén (Wrong: Read as "Tiā - nān - mén")
→
Tiān'ānmén (Correct: Clear "Tian" & "an")

✍️ Rule #5: Word Glue & Big Letters

📐 How to Count Chinese Syllables:

In Mandarin, 1 Chinese Character = 1 Spoken Syllable = 1 Pinyin Unit. Every single syllable you speak counts as 1 syllable!

Wǒ(1) bà(2)ba(3) mā(4)ma(5) hěn(6) máng(7). → 7 Syllables total!
